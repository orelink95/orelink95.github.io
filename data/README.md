# Site data

> **These files are the only copy of the site's places, people, library and campaigns.**
> Edit them with the dashboard: open `admin_tools/dashboard.html` in Chrome, Edge or Brave (double-clicking the file works), link the site's root folder once (next time: **Reopen**), edit, then **Save** (Ctrl+S). It refuses to save while a link is broken. You can also edit the JSON by hand and check it with `node tools/validate.js`; if the dashboard is open, press its **Reload from disk** button first, or its next save overwrites your hand edits.
> The site's pages fetch these files, so view them through a local server (VS Code Live Server), not from `file://`.

One file per type. Every record has an `id` (never renamed), a `name` and optional `aliases`.
`data/schema.js` declares every field; `node tools/validate.js` checks the files against it.

- **Hidden from players.** `"hidden": true` on any record keeps it off the site until you reveal it: no page, no card in the lists, no map pin, no automatic link (its name stays plain text), no preview card. What depends on it is hidden with it: the places inside a hidden place (at any depth), the NPCs whose `home` is hidden, the handouts and sessions of a hidden campaign, any record whose required link points to a hidden one (a card of a hidden deck, a rumour heard only in a hidden tavern…), and a record listed only in hidden campaigns. Links from visible records to hidden ones are left out on the site (a fighter, a group member, a quote, a notable…). In the dashboard: **Visible / Hidden** at the top of every form, a *Hidden from players* box in the new-record dialog, a crossed eye in the lists and the tree, and the **Hidden from players** card of Home, with a **Reveal** button for each. Its warnings list every visible record that links to a hidden one, or names it in its text (the name would still show, unlinked). Hidden records are still in the public JSON files: anyone reading the files on GitHub can see them. **DM preview**: add `?drafts=1` to a page's address (the dashboard's *View on site* does it on a hidden record) to see the site with hidden records, for the rest of the browser tab; it only works where `data/private.json` loads, i.e. on your own copy, never on the live site. A tag in the corner says it is on; click it (or `?drafts=0`) to go back to the players' view.
- **The bottom of the homepage** (`index.html`, `scripts/latest-updates.js`; dashboard: Site › Homepage). Each card of **Latest Updates** links to a `record` (any type with a page: its page, name and picture, i.e. its first picture field, else the map of one of its parts) or is a card of your own (`title`, `image`, `link`); `title`, `image` and `link` also win over the record's. Under the title: `caption`, else “Added 3 days ago” counted from `added` (a real date). A card whose record is hidden from players, or deleted, is left out; no card = no section. From any record's form, **⋯ › Show in Latest updates** puts it first, dated today. `allLabel` / `allLink`: the link beside the heading (empty = View All Scrolls, the scriptorium). `quote` and `quoteBy`: the closing quote (empty = none).
- **Links are ids.** Write `"home": "gilded-goldfinch"`, never a display name. Lists such as a POI's residents or a faction's members are worked out by the site.
- **Map pins live on the child.** `map: {x, y, icon}` places a record on its parent's map. Only continents have a `layer` (the layer buttons of the world map). A settlement has no map of its own (its maps are its districts'), so a POI sitting directly in a settlement has no pin.
- **Long text is Markdown.** Blank line = new paragraph; `**bold**`, `*italic*`, `[text](url)`, `> quote` with `> — Name` on the last line. `{text}` keeps a name from becoming an automatic link (the braces are not shown): `{Caeraotrom}`. Put the braces inside any bold or italics: `**{Caeraotrom}**`.
- **A tavern is a POI with a `tavern` block** (tier, picks, signature items, services). Its staff are the NPCs whose `home` is that POI. Its page is `poi.html?id=<poi id>`.
- **Tavern menus change with `tavernCode`** in `settings.json` (the market code): same code, same menu, same wares and same rumour order for every player. Preview another code with `poi.html?id=…&code=XYZ` (not saved).
- **One POI, several roles.** A POI can have a `tavern`, a `shop` and a `temple` block at once (a tavern that is also a general store: fill in both). Each role adds a tab to its page — Map, Locations, People (institutions, below), Fare (`tavern`), Games (`gaming`), The Pit (`pit`), Wares (`shop`), Workbench (`alchemist`), Shrine (`temple`), Readings (`divination`), Rumours, Board — and on phones the tabbed card comes right under the picture, its tabs stuck under the header. `poi.html?id=…#wares` opens a tab (`#map`, `#places`, `#people`, `#fare`, `#games`, `#pit`, `#wares`, `#bench`, `#shrine`, `#readings`, `#rumours`, `#board`).
- **Institutions** (a university, a great library). A POI with an `institution` block gets three more tabs, and a wider card for them:
  - **Map**: `institution.maps`, one per floor, wing or ground (`id`, `name`, `image` with its width and height, optional `note` shown under the map). Several maps = a picker above the map.
  - **Locations**: its key locations are ordinary POIs whose `parent` is the institution, so each has its own page, lore, residents, and can itself be a tavern, a shop or a temple. Pin one with `map: {x, y, icon, on}`, where `on` is the id of one of the institution's maps (none = the first map). A location with no pin is listed under “Elsewhere”.
  - **People**: `institution.groups` (`name`, `desc`, `members`: an `npc` and the `title` they hold there; no title = the NPC's own role). The same NPC can be in several groups. Whoever has their `home` in the institution or one of its locations and is in no group comes last, under “Also Found Here”. With no group at all, the tab lists everyone who lives there by location (the institution itself first). On an institution page the People tab replaces the residents list of the left column.
  - **POIs list** (`pois.html`): a POI that sits in another POI has no card of its own there. The card of the place that holds it says “N places within”, and a name search still finds it.
  - `institution.kind` (the `institutionKind` enum in `data/schema.js`) sets the wording: the label in the header, the headings of the three tabs and the map pop-up button. Add a kind there (academy, monastery…) to get its own wording.
  - Links: `poi.html?id=<institution>#map`, `#places`, `#people`; `&map=<map id>` opens a map and `&spot=<location id>` opens its map on its pin. A location's own page links back to its pin (“Within”).
  - In the dashboard: the **Institution** card of the POI; its side panel lists the places inside it with the map each is pinned on, and **+ Place inside this one** creates one. On a location, the pin field has a **Map** choice and *Place on the map* opens that map.
- **Shops.** `shop.kind` sets the shelves (`shopCategory`, 35 of them, from Breads to Funerary Goods and Trades & Services). There are 33 kinds: food (bakery, pastry shop, sweet shop, grocer, butcher, fishmonger, cheesemonger, spice & tea merchant, wine merchant, market stall), clothes and finery (tailor, draper, cobbler, jeweller, perfumer, barber-surgeon), arms (smithy, bowyer), craft and home (potter, chandler, florist, toymaker, instrument maker, tobacconist, pet shop), learning and magic (alchemist, arcane emporium, scribe, curiosity shop), plus general store, stable, undertaker and fence. A kind's `shelfTags` narrows a shelf to goods with one of its tags: a barber's Trades & Services shelf shows only goods tagged `barber`. Kinds and shelves are edited in `data/schema.js` (`enums`); each shelf shows `picks` seeded goods (default 5) from `shop-goods.json` that the tier and tags allow, plus the shop's `signature` wares. `keepers` names the shopkeeper(s), shown at the top of the Wares tab: any NPC, so a POI with several residents (a tavern that is also a store) can say who minds the shop. Taverns (`tavern.keepers`, “Behind the bar”) and temples (`temple.keepers`, “Tending the altar”) have the same field. Price = `basePrice` × tier multiplier × `markup`%. With no `tier`, a shop takes its tavern's. Goods tagged `illicit` are only sold by fences (or a shop whose `includeTags` has `illicit`).
- **Temples.** `temple.deities` (Deity records, shown in the Pantheon, `pantheon.html`), `services` from `temple-services.json` (prices × the temple's tier) and `customServices` (no price = “Donation”). “Pray” draws one blessing at random from `blessings.json` (those with no `deities`, or one of this temple's); each player gets one per temple until the market code changes. `noPrayer` hides the button.
- **The Pantheon.** `pantheon.html` shows every god as a card, one section per `tier` (The Creators, The Great Pantheon, Minor Gods, Primordials, Elder Gods: the `deityTier` enum, in page order); a card opens the god's page, `deity.html?id=…` (old `pantheon.html?id=…` links still work). Each tier has its own frame (`scripts/deity-card.js`, `styles/deity-card.css`): give a god a `portrait` (full body, upright 2:3; `portraitZoom` zooms into it, in %, centred on `portraitFocus`, e.g. `50% 20%`; with no zoom the whole portrait shows), a `symbol` (a PNG with a transparent background, shown in the medallion) and a `colour` (the gems and the glow). `artifact` is an item: the god's page tells it as a legend (`artifactLegend`), and it reaches the Vault only once a session's loot or a party's inventory lists it, like any item; until then its name is not a keyword link either. **Elder gods are sealed**: a nameless slot in the Pantheon, a sealed page, no keyword link, left out of their temples, until their `revealedIn` lists the campaign. The Pantheon has the campaign drop-down of Dramatis Personae; other pages follow the campaign last picked in the browser tab (else `currentCampaign`). In the dashboard, a god's side panel previews its card (Revealed / Sealed for an Elder god) and lists, campaign by campaign, where it is revealed.
- **Divination.** A POI with a `divination` block is a seer: its page gets a **Readings** tab (icon an eye) with who reads (`keepers`, “Reading the signs”), the practices and their prices (`basePrice` × the seer's `tier`, else the tavern's, else Modest), and a **Seek a reading** button. The reading opens in a pop-up (full screen on phones): the cards lie face down, a tap turns one, its meaning appears under the spread; a tap on a turned card shows it large. **One reading per player, per seer, per day** (the browser's calendar day; in each campaign of the drop-down); it stays there, cards turned, until the next day.
  - **Practices** (`practices.json`): `method` *cards* draws from a `deck` into a spread the player picks among the practice's `spreads` (none = every spread); *omen* draws one record of `omens.json` whose `practice` is this one (bone casting, tea leaves…: `text`, `meaning`, a square `image`, else the practice's `icon`).
  - **Decks and cards**: a card (`fortune-cards.json`) has a `deck`, a `number` (0 = The Fool; its order, and the numeral of a card with no picture), an `image` (the whole face, tarot size 70 × 120, upright), `keywords` and `upright`, plus `reversedKeywords` and `reversed` (empty = the upright meaning, marked reversed). A drawn card is reversed one time in two unless its deck has `noReversals`. A deck's `back` is the back of all its cards (none = a patterned back), so a deck works before its art is ready.
  - **Spreads** (`spreads.json`): `positions` (`name`, `desc`), laid in a row (three per line on phones), or as a cross when `layout` is *cross* and there are five (1 centre, 2 left, 3 right, 4 above, 5 below), or anywhere when `layout` is *free*: each position has an `x` (column) and `y` (row) on a grid of card-sized cells (decimals allowed), an optional `tilt` (degrees) and `colour` (its name and a halo around its card). A free spread shrinks as one on phones, where only the numerals show above the cards; it falls back to a row if a position has no `x` / `y`. Eraldom spreads: The Two Moons, The Judgement of Souls, The Dreamlands Thread, The Eight Days, The Mobius Loop, The Void Between.
  - **Prophecies** (`prophecies.json`): a reading fixed by the DM. `where` (the seer), `campaigns`, the `practice` (one the seer offers), the `spread` and the `cards` in its order (`card`, `reversed`, and `reading`: what the seer reads in it, else the card's own meaning), or `omen` / `omenMeaning` for an omen practice; `intro` and `closing` are said before and after. A player of one of its campaigns gets it at their next reading there, whatever they asked for (marked “The threads were already drawn”); several = in file order, one a day; then readings go back to random. `meaning` (what it really foretells) is DM-only (`private.json`).
  - **The seer's interpretation**: once every card is turned (never for a prophecy, whose own words stand), the seer reads the spread as a whole, in the first person: one of the spread's `openings`; each card in its position (one of the position's `frames`, with the card's `essence` / `reversedEssence` slotted in for `{essence}` and its name for `{card}`); at most two **reading rules** (`reading-rules.json`) whose pattern is in the spread — `card` (one of its `cards`, at a `position`, drawn a given way) and `together` (all its cards) first, then every card reversed / most reversed / all upright, numbers rising / falling (3+ cards); the `counsel` of the card in the spread's `keyPosition` (else the last); and one closing among the rules `bright` / `mixed` / `dark`, chosen by the cards' `tone` (`reversedTone` empty = one step darker). A rule can be limited to a `deck` and to `spreads`. Choices among variants are seeded by the reading, so a reload says the same. A card with no essence falls back on its keywords. Texts are a first pass, per deck, from the placeholder meanings.
  - **Casts** (`method` *cast*): the practice throws `castCount` pieces (empty = all) of its `pieceSet` (`piece-sets.json`: `look` stones / bones, `pieceColour`, `glyphColour`, `glyphGlow`, `noReversals`) on a round cloth whose `rings` (centre outwards, equal widths; `name`, `desc`, `frames`; empty = The heart / Near / Far) say what each piece speaks of. Where every piece falls is drawn and kept at the draw; the player throws it on screen (**Cast the stones**), then touches each face to read it. About a third land face down (silent); pieces beyond the cloth are not read; a piece can land merkstave (reversed) unless it is `symmetric` or its set has `noReversals`. A piece (`cast-pieces.json`) has a `glyph` (an SVG path in a 10 × 16 box, stroked) or an `image` (black on transparent, tinted with the glyph colour), `keywords`, `meaning`, `reversed…`, and the same reading fields as a card. The interpretation works as for cards: the practice's `openings`, each face read in its ring's `frames` (no frame repeated while others remain), cast rules (`heartEmpty`, `mostDown`, `twoReversed`, `offCloth`, `allDown`, plus `card` — `position` = the ring — and `together`), the counsel of the face nearest the centre, a closing (rules with `method` *cast*). **The cloth is the seer's**: `clothColour`, `clothImage`, `ringColour` on the POI's divination block, else the practice's. Prophecies never fix a cast.
  - In the dashboard: the **Divination** card of a POI; its side panel shows the prices and the prophecies waiting there (**+ Prophecy read here**). A card previews upright, reversed and its back; a deck shows its cards (**+ Card in this deck** numbers the new one); a spread and a prophecy preview as they are laid; a spread also shows **a sample reading** with its interpretation (a deck to pick, **Draw again**), and a card how the seer says it in each position. Markup: `scripts/fortune-card.js` and `styles/fortune-card.css`; draws: `scripts/divination.js`.
- **The planes of existence** (`planes.html`): an orrery drawn from `planes.json`, by each plane's `kind`. *At the centre*: the first two share the disc, cut along the diagonal (the first upper right). *Laid over*: a dashed veil around the disc, its medallion on the side of the plane in `over`. *A ring*: a band around the centre, its name written along it. *On a circle*: a medallion orbiting on circle `circle` (1 = nearest), starting at `angle` (0 = top, clockwise; empty = spread evenly); a circle turns as one, the farther the slower. *Beyond the circles*: a band around the whole orrery, its medallion at `angle`; with `touchesAll`, hovering or choosing it runs threads of its colour to every plane. *Beneath the centre*: a dark ring with embers just outside the veil (the Underworld; one). *Between the planes*: the dark field between the circles (one). Any plane off the circles may have `roads` (to, colour) to planes on the circles: hovered or chosen, they light up with souls flowing out (the Underworld's roads to Aran and Tartarus). *Demiplane*: a card under the orrery (`anchor`, `way`). Each plane has a `colour`, an `icon` (Material Symbols) or an `iconImage` (an SVG, black on a transparent background, is filled with the colour; another picture is shown as it is) and `glow` (a halo of its colour). Hovering a medallion holds the orrery still; choosing a plane opens it in the panel (`planes.html?plane=<id>`) with its `summary` and `links`. **Sealed planes** (`sealed` + `revealedIn`, like Elder gods): a locked medallion with no name on a circle or beyond them, a sealed card among the demiplanes, not drawn at all elsewhere. The page has the campaign drop-down of Dramatis Personae. “Enter the plane” opens its own page.
- **A plane's page** (`plane.html?id=<plane>`, scripts/plane-scripts.js): medallion (hovered, it breathes), name, epithet, summary, then `traits` (your labels), the lore (`body`) with `ways` in and “Spoken of in” (`links`, plus what links to the plane), its **maps** (`maps`: one tab each, Leaflet) with its **places** pinned on them (`places`: name, other names, `pin` {x, y, icon, on = the map's id}, picture, summary; placed in the dashboard with “Place on the map”), **who dwells there** (`groups` of `members`: name, title, a deity or not, portrait, text), with `atlas` (the Material Plane), **the world**: the world map leading to the Grand Atlas and its continents; the **denizens** (creatures whose `realm` is the plane's name or one of its other names) and the planes next to it on the orrery. Places and residents live only on this page, but their names (and other names) link to it from the whole site (`#place-<key>` opens the map on that place, `#who-<key>` shows the resident), with preview cards; their key is their `id`, else made from the name (scripts/core/store.js, `planePart`). Planes themselves link too (jade, `kw-plane`). Sealed: a sealed plane shows only “A sealed plane” and its neighbours; a sealed place is not pinned nor linked; a sealed resident is “Not yet met” (each with its own `revealedIn`). **Its look**: `look` as on POI and creature pages, with the planeTheme themes: colours and fonts in styles/looks.css (`.look--plane-<id>`), and what the theme draws around the page (canopy, shards…) in scripts/plane-themes.js. No theme: the orrery's starry sky. Ydalheim's theme: Verdant Wilds.
- **Rumours.** A rumour's `where` can be a POI (that tavern only) or any place above it (a settlement's rumours are heard in all its taverns). A tavern tells its rumours in a seeded order (per market code); each player hears the first `rumourDraws` (default 3) that belong to their campaign, one per “Buy the room a round”. Their draws are kept in their browser and start again when the code changes. `truth` and `note` are DM-only (`private.json`). In the dashboard, a tavern's side panel lists its rumours in order (✓ = heard in the current campaign).
- **Gaming table.** A POI's `gaming` block (card **Gaming table**) gives its page a Games tab; on a tavern it also adds a free “Gaming table” line to its services, and without a `tavern` block the place is a gambling den. `games` ticks the games on the table (enum `tavernGame`: Liar's Dice, Knucklebones, Crown and Dagger). Players play in the browser against bots: the NPCs whose home is the place first, then generic regulars. They name their stakes (*Low / Standard / High*, enum `gameStakes`, base blinds 5 / 10 / 50 cp) or play for fun. The **blind** is the stakes' base blind × the table's `tier` (else the tavern's, else Modest); every seat puts one in the pot each hand and the winner takes it. Crown and Dagger also has a *Double Down* (one more blind into the pot, matched or folded by each regular). The table can drop the cheapest stakes (`minStake`), keep a share of every pot (`houseCut`, 0–50 %) and lock its highest stakes until a player has won `highTableWins` staked hands there. The **starting purse** is `startingPurse` in `settings.json` (Settings → Gaming purse; empty = 500 cp); the purse, like the won/lost tally, is kept in each player's browser, and changing the number gives everyone a fresh purse. A player who can't cover the lowest blind borrows a starting purse from the house at `loanInterest` %; half of every win repays it. The dashboard's POI view previews the blinds of its tier. To add a game: a file in `scripts/games/` that calls `Athenaeum.games.register()` (contract in the header of `scripts/games/table.js`), loaded by `poi.html`, plus its id in the `tavernGame` enum.
- **Fighting pit.** A POI's `pit` block (card **Fighting pit**) gives its page a **The Pit** tab (`scripts/pit.js`, `styles/pit.css`): a card of bouts each fight night, which players back with their gaming purse before watching the fight. `keepers` (the Pitmaster), `nights` (weekday names of the calendar; empty = every night), `bouts` a night (1–6, default 3, fewer if fighters run short), `houseCut` (% taken from what a winning bet earns, default 10), `maxWager` (gp; empty = only the purse). `fighters`: each an `npc`, a `creature`, or a house fighter (`name`, plus `image` and `tag` if you like; a `name` on an NPC or creature is a ring name); `strength` 1–10 sets the odds and the fight (empty = from a creature's threat, else 5), `wins` / `losses` show on the card. Tonight is the date of the world (`calendar.now`, else the latest session); the card is drawn from the market code, the POI and that date, so every player sees the same bouts. **Fixed fights** (`pitFixes`, DM only, `private.json`): on that night, that fighter (its name as it shows on the card) wins every bout it is in; the odds don't show it. In the dashboard the POI's side panel shows a night's card as players will see it (pick another date to check ahead).
- **Lists with an `enum`** in the schema (tavern games, shop shelves) are ticked as checkboxes in the dashboard; the validator rejects ids not in the enum.
- **Page looks** (POIs and creatures). The **Look** card of a POI or a creature changes that one page: a `theme` (POIs and creatures each have their own: the `poiTheme` and `creatureTheme` enums), then any of `accent`, `ink` (text), `panel` (boxes), `card` (a POI's tabbed card), `page` (background) as `#rrggbb` colours, a `texture` (or `image` + `textureImage`) and its `textureStrength` (%), a `titleFont` / `bodyFont` (`lookFont` enum, fetched from Google Fonts only where used), a `frame` for the boxes (`lookFrame`), and a `banner` image fading out behind the top of the page (`bannerFocus`, `bannerStrength` %). An empty field keeps the theme's value, or the site's. On a POI, **Also for the places inside** (`inherit`) passes the whole look down to the POIs that sit in it, at any depth, unless they have a look of their own. The themes, textures and frames are CSS, in `styles/looks.css`; to change a theme everywhere, edit it there. The dashboard previews the look in the side panel as you edit.
- **Notice boards.** A notice's `where` is a settlement (its Town Board, under the map) or a POI (its Board tab). `kind` sets the look (wanted posters have a picture), `status` stamps it Taken or Closed and sends it to the end. Like NPCs, `campaigns` (none = every campaign) decides who sees it; the POI and settlement pages share the campaign drop-down of Dramatis Personae. A POI's or settlement's side panel in the dashboard has **+ Rumour** / **+ Notice** buttons that create one already pinned there.
- **NPCs somewhere off the map** (another plane…) have no `home`; put a line of text in `whereabouts` instead.
- **Keywords.** Names of NPCs, places, factions and creatures become links on their own. A term with no page (a war, a lost civilisation) is a glossary term in `lore.json`; its `kind` sets the colour and its `definition` the tooltip in the scriptorium. Give an NPC, faction or place a `summary` to show a tooltip for it too.
- **Sigil Maker and folios.** `sigil-maker.html` is the magic circle generator: players can design circles there, export them (PNG, SVG, the whole book as PDF or PNG) and save a **recipe** (a .json file holding every ring). Its engine is `scripts/sigil-engine.js`, shared with the dashboard. A **Folio** (Tables › Generators › Folios in the dashboard) is one of those books with your own words: import a recipe in its previews panel, then fill in any text of the two pages (title, subtitle, running heads, margin note, the text with `*italic*` and `**small capitals**`, the rubric, the table, the plate heading and caption) and, in **Book**, the flavour of the generated text (Latin grimoire, or an arcane tome with a D&D table: school, level, casting time, components, duration, risk; it names nothing of the world but the schools of magic), the school, and the colours of the cover and of the illuminated initial (12 each; empty = the ones picked in the Sigil Maker, saved in the recipe). An empty text field keeps the generated text; a single `-` leaves that part blank. The previews panel draws the book as it will print and exports it (PDF as an A3 spread or two A4 pages, the book as a PNG, the circle alone, the recipe). Folios are **DM only**: the type has `dmOnly` in the schema, so `data/folios.json` is git-ignored and the site never fetches it. Alphabets uploaded in the Sigil Maker travel inside the recipe, so the dashboard draws them too.
- **Hero secrets** are written in `private.json` (`secret` and `password`). The dashboard encrypts them into `sealedSecret` when it saves, so the live site shows a secret only to someone who types its password.
- **Loot cards.** Every item is a card in the Artifact Vault (`vault.html`). Players see the items listed in their campaign's sessions (`loot`) and the party's own (`inventory`). Fill in `type`, `rarity` (sets the card's colour), `cost`, `weight`, `attunement`, `attunedBy`, a one-line `summary` for the front and a full `body` for the opened card. Items from the newest session lie face down until a player turns them over. In the dashboard, **+ New** on Items asks where the party found it (a session's loot or a party's inventory) and the side panel previews the card, front and opened. The card's markup and styles are shared: `scripts/loot-card.js` and `styles/loot-card.css`.
- **Spell cards.** Every spell is a card in the Tome of Shadows (`spellbook.html`): pick a campaign, then a hero. What a hero knows is written on the **campaign**, in `spellbooks` (one entry per hero: `hero` + `spells`), so a hero in two campaigns can know different spells in each. Fill in `level` (0 = cantrip, shown as C), `school` (sets the card's colour and symbols), `castingTime`, `range`, `duration`, the `concentration`, `ritual`, `verbal`, `somatic` and `material` boxes, `materials` (the components: shown when hovering the M, which is then marked with a star), a one-line `summary` for the card and a full `body` for the opened card. The same cards open from a hero's card on `heroes.html`. A spell a browser has never turned over lies face down (its back takes the school's colour); the first click turns it, the next opens it. In the dashboard, the side panel previews the card, its back and the opened card. Markup and styles: `scripts/spell-card.js` and `styles/spell-card.css`; school symbols and the back's art (`card-back.png`) in `images/spells/`.
- **The world's calendar** (`calendar.json`, Site › Calendar): the 13 `months` and their `days`, the `leap` day (`month` gets one more day every `every` years, counted from `year`, a known leap year: 1690, 1693, 1696…), the `weekdays` in order (the week runs on across months and years), and `anchor` + `anchorWeekday`, one date whose weekday is known: every other weekday is worked out from it. `era` (LF) follows every year; `eraName` is the era in full; `before` names the time before year 1 (The Old Days). `now` sets the year now of the timeline (empty = the date of the latest session). `seasons` follow one another from Arawal 1 (`days`, `colour`; the last one takes what is left: winter is 108 days in a leap year, 107 otherwise). `moons` have a `cycle` (days from full to full), a `colour` and one `full` date: every phase is counted from it (Mel’Tara 33 days, full Arawal 29, 1693; Ior’Tara 196 days, full Pholawal 6, 1693). Code: `scripts/core/calendar.js` (`store.calendar.format`, `compare`, `weekday`…), loaded before the store on the pages that show dates.
- **World dates** (`kind: 'worlddate'`): `{ "year": 1693, "month": "Pholawal", "day": 7 }`; month and day are optional (`{ "year": 1341 }`), `"approx": true` writes “c.”. The weekday is never stored: it is worked out (“Eksten 7 Pholawal, 1693 LF”). A session's `date` is one; empty = unknown. The dashboard edits them as day / month / year / “about”, the weekday shown beside them.
- **The Book of Days**, the timeline (`timeline.html`, Chronicle › Book of Days). An event has a `date` (and an `end` for a war, a reign…), or is `oldDays` with an `order` (1 = the oldest). `kind`: *event* (a panel, alternating sides), *major* (a wide red panel across the line), *age* (a round seal: the Leviathans’ Fall). `summary` is always shown; `body`, `image` and `links` (places, people, factions, gods… with their preview cards) when it is opened. **Hidden history**: `sealed` events are a sealed page until a campaign lists them in `revealedIn` (`store.revealed`, like the Elder gods). An event can have its own `colour` (panel, line of light, node) and `icon` (a Material Symbols name) or `iconImage` (your own picture; an SVG is tinted in the event's colour) on the line in place of the star. The page shows, newest first: the year now, with the sessions of the campaigns the reader shows (none by default: one chip per campaign, remembered for the visit; a session with no date sits after the dated one before it in its campaign), the events, then the Old Days; a stretch of 50 years or more with no event becomes “some N years of silence”. “Viewing as” (the campaign, shared with Dramatis Personae) decides what is sealed; hovering a panel sends a line of energy around its border; `timeline.html?event=<id>` opens an event. The sky behind it: `scripts/core/sky.js`. Styles: `styles/pages/timeline.css`.
- **The calendar** (the Book of Days' *Calendar* tab: `timeline.html?view=calendar`, `&date=1693-Pholawal-7`, `&festival=<id>`; script `scripts/astrolabe-calendar.js`). A month in 8 columns (the weekdays): each day with its moons in their phase, “Full Mel’” / “New Ior’”, the first day of a season, its festivals and its history events; today glows; the panel beside it tells the chosen day and what is coming. **Festivals** (`festivals.json`, Chronicle › Festivals): `on` (`{month, day}`, every year; Arawal 32 only in leap years), `days` (a span), `kind` (festival, holy day, market or fair, day of remembrance: their colour), `summary`, `body`, `deity`, `places`, `campaigns` (empty = every campaign). **History events** with a month and a day are on their day; with `yearly` (“Remembered every year”) on that day every year after, with “N years ago”. Hidden history and campaign-only festivals follow the campaign chosen on the Timeline tab.
- **Today in Eraldom** (homepage card, `scripts/today-widget.js`): today's date, season (with how far into it), moons and the next festival (known in the current campaign of the settings); it opens the calendar. It reads only `calendar.json`, `festivals.json`, `sessions.json` and `settings.json`.
- **Upcoming sessions** (homepage cards) are `sessions` in `schedule.json`, shown in file order: `campaign`, `number`, `date` (YYYY-MM-DD), `time` (HH:MM), optional `status` (`tentative` = purple TBD card, `postponed` = amber with the date struck through, `cancelled` = red and dimmed; none = confirmed), `notice` (a message on the card), `title`, `where`, `link`. A date that has passed is hidden on its own; the first confirmed card still to come is “Up Next”, with a countdown. Confirmed cards have an **Add to calendar** button (an .ics event lasting `length` hours, default 4, with a reminder 2 hours before). In the dashboard (Site › Upcoming sessions), **+ Add a session** prefills the next number, date and time; the side panel previews the homepage and can sort by date or remove past sessions.
- **NPCs and factions per campaign.** An NPC lists its campaigns in `campaigns` (one entry per campaign: `campaign` + the party's `relation` there), so the same NPC can be an ally in one campaign and hostile in another. A faction belongs to the campaigns whose `standings` list it (`faction` + `status`). One listed in no campaign is part of every campaign, with its own `relation` (NPC) or `status` (faction). `npc.html`, `heroes.html` and `factions.html` share one campaign drop-down (`scripts/personae.js`); it opens on `currentCampaign` from `settings.json`, and NPCs and Factions also offer *All campaigns*. Pages ask the store: `store.inCampaign(record, campaignId)` and `store.standing(record, campaignId)`.
- **The Scriptorium** (`scriptorium.html?campaign=…`, `scripts/scriptorium-scripts.js`): a campaign's sessions, newest first, grouped by `arc`, with the next planned session (from `schedule.json`) on top; `?session=<id>` opens a session's folio. A session's `places` (records) draw the **Journey** tab (`scripts/scriptorium-journey.js`: each place where it, or the nearest place above it, is pinned, numbered in the order first reached); `otherPlaces` are names with no page. The **Story** tab is the campaign's `chapters`: one per arc, written exactly as in the sessions' `arc`, each a list of `passages` (`text`, and the `sessions` that tell it: the note in its margin). Under the title, a random one of the campaign's `quotes` (`text`, said `by` a hero or an NPC, else `name`) and **Previously on…**, the latest session's `recap` (empty = its summary).
- **Drafts.** A tome or handout with `"draft": true` stays off the site.
- **Check your edits** with `node tools/validate.js` (broken links, unknown values, missing images and map icons).
- **Map icons** (Site › Map icons, `map-icons.json`): the pictures of the map pins, `images/atlas_icons/<id>.svg`. A pin names one in `map.icon` and picks it in a visual picker. The gallery shows each picture and how many pins use it; **New icon** takes an SVG, PNG or WebP file or pasted SVG code (an SVG is cleaned of scripts, a PNG or WebP is wrapped in an SVG); an icon can be replaced, renamed (its pins follow) or deleted (refused while a pin uses it). Pictures are written or deleted on save. `cardIcon` is the Material Symbols icon on the back of the places' cards in the Atlas list pages (empty = `location_on`).
- **The dashboard's forms come from the schema.** Each field's `label` (short), `help`, `section` (the card it sits in; a type lists its cards in `sections`), `width` and `showIf` (shown only when another field has a value) are read by the dashboard only. Where a type sits in the dashboard is its `workspace`: **Atlas** (every place in one tree: *+ … here* adds a child, drag to reorder siblings), **Cast** (NPCs, factions, heroes, deities), **Chronicle** (campaigns, sessions, events, festivals, rumours, notices), **Codex** (glossary, tomes, handouts, planes, creatures, spells, items), **Tables** (the pools, by `group`, each a grid whose `columns` are edited in place; a row opens its full form in a drawer), and **Site** behind the gear (world, settings, calendar, upcoming sessions, map icons); **Home** shows the next sessions, quick add, unsaved records, problems and recent records. The campaign switcher in the top bar filters NPCs, heroes, factions, sessions, handouts, rumours and notices, and sets the campaign of new sessions, rumours and notices. A POI's optional blocks are added with **+ Role** (and **+ Look**). The ⋯ menu of a record renames its id (every link follows, on save), moves it, or deletes it (refused while something still points to it). Items and spells have a **Print** button: physical cards at tarot size (70 × 120 mm), backs mirrored for double-sided printing. Shortcuts: **Ctrl K** finds any record or tool, **Alt ↑ / ↓** opens the previous / next one, **Ctrl S** saves, **Esc** closes a dialog. A gold dot marks what is not saved yet. `admin_tools/dashboard.html?test`, served by Live Server, reads the files over HTTP and keeps every write in memory: a safe place to try a schema change.
- **Potions** (`scripts/alchemy-engine.js`). A potion is a vessel and three reagents: a solvent, an active principle and a catalyst. Each reagent has a property (`tag`); the active principle reacts with the solvent and with the catalyst through the Reaction records (one per pair of properties; three reagents sharing a property use that property's triad instead).
  - **Players** meet potions on POI pages only, and never see what one does (`effect`, `modifier`, a reaction's `effect`):
    - **Shops**: `shop.potions` (a number) shelves that many potions on the Wares tab, drawn with the market code, up to `shop.potionRarity` (default Uncommon), optionally only from `shop.potionPrinciples`. Players see the bottle, the name and the price (the value × the shop's price level).
    - **Alchemists**: a POI with an `alchemist` block gets a Workbench tab. Players pick a vessel and one of each reagent among what it stocks (`vessels`, empty = all; `solvents`, `principles`, `catalysts`) and see only the craft DC, the tool, the price (`fee` % of the value, default 50) and a six-letter **brew mark** they read out to you.
  - **You**: the dashboard shows each shop's potions with their effects (**Make it a card…**), and each Workbench's stock; a brew mark typed in **Brew a potion** (or under the Workbench box) finds the mix. **Brew a potion** on an item writes the card: name, type, rarity, cost, weight, both descriptions. The description is what players read once the card is in their Vault, so edit it freely.
  - **The potion maker** (`admin_tools/potion-maker.html`, opened from the dashboard's Alchemy page through the live server) shows any potion with its effect, value, craft DC and brew mark, and keeps a shelf in the browser. Players reach a version of it from **The Scribe's Guild** in the footer (`potion-maker.html?guild`): bottle, name, look and the reagents' lore only, with a shelf of its own. The Sigil Maker (`sigil-maker.html`) sits in the same pop-up.
  - A potion card keeps only `potion: {seed, recipe, studio}`; its bottle is drawn from that wherever the card shows (Vault, dashboard). Deleting a reagent or vessel a card uses leaves it without a bottle: `node tools/validate.js` and the dashboard warn about it.
  - Rarity comes from the active principle (a catalyst two grades above it lifts it one step). Value = the 2024 DMG value halved for a consumable (50 / 200 / 2,000 / 20,000 gp), +10% per catalyst grade, ×1.2 per synergy, ×0.8 per adverse reaction. Craft DC = 8 / 11 / 14 / 17 by rarity, plus each reagent's `dcMod`, +1 per synergy, +2 per adverse reaction.
  - A new property: add it to the `potionTag` list in `data/schema.js`, then give it a glyph in Alchemy. The engine draws something special for the ten it knows; a new one works, undrawn.
